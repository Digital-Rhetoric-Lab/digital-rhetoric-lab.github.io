Module 2: Inquiry into Digital Network Issues

This module will prepare you for Module 3. It will help you identify a specific issue, understand why it matters, who is affected, use sources to learn more, and point toward a possible intervention or prototype.

Explore

In Module 1, you wrote about your own digital literacy moments. In this module, you’ll connect those experiences to bigger issues in the digital world. A personal experience can raise larger questions about audience, trust, algorithms, access, identity, privacy, activism, AI, or how information moves online.

As you explore these issues, you may focus on your own experience, a group/community you belong to, a field you’re interested in, or a broader population affected by digital networks.

Research

Identify the Problem

Which part of the digital environment seems connected to the problem?

Write

Introduce the digital network issue you explored. Briefly explain what the issue is and why it matters, and who is affected by it.
Writing tip: give your readers enough background to understand the issue.
Start with a topic sentence that clearly names the issue and summarizes what your sources show about it. This sentence should come from what you learned by reading your sources, not just from a personal opinion you already had. Ask yourself: what do my sources show me about this issue?
Explain the main aspects you learned from the sources. For each aspect, use specific examples, findings, or evidence from those sources to help your readers understand the point.
Writing tip: The “main aspects” are the key parts of the issue that you name in your topic sentence. After you name them, use the rest of the paragraph to explain each aspect with examples or evidence from your sources. Evidence can be an example, research findings, statistic, expert explanation, or specific case from your sources.
End the report by explaining what problem, need, question, or opportunity you report reveals. Ask yourself: What issue still needs attention? What could be improved, clarified, redesigned, explained, or supported? What kind of intervention might help a specific audience?
